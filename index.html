What goes into
every answer

    
On top of the person's message, Claude gets five layers. Two are about the person, three are what we taught Dassie.

    

      
Always
Our rulesHow to write, safety, what Dassie is. Written by the team. The length rule (50–120 characters) is not based on research.

      
Every night
LessonsShort rules learned from yesterday's chats: what helped, what didn't.

      
Per message
1 topic guide12 guides (focus, sleep, burnout, work…) with 51 research links. Only 1 of 12 has been checked, and by an AI.

      
Per message
Their notebook pagesPeople, routines, goals, and how they like to be answered.

      
Per person
A private hintFrom reading their patterns (below). Never said out loud.

Reading people: ADHD, autism, both, or unclear

        

          
1
They talk"I can't start anything until 4pm." Or they say it: "I have ADHD."

          
2
Dassie takes notesUp to 12 observations in their own words. Saying it counts as strong evidence.

          
3

4

When ADHD fits"favour task-initiation help (one tiny, concrete first step), externalised time (timers, reminders, exact times) and short chunks; offer to stay with them while they start."

          
When autism fits"favour literal, predictable wording: say what happens next, ask one clear question at a time, avoid vague open questions; keep sensory and social load low and respect their routines."

Why the reply gets more expensive every day

        
Claude rereads the whole chat with every reply. The chat grows by about 500 tokens per message and is only summarised at 700,000 tokens, about day 136 at 10 messages a day.

        
If the person answers within 5 minutes, the chat is read from cache, which is cheap. After a pause it is written again at full price: that "cold" reply is what costs.
